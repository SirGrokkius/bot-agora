## What a row is
Each record is one tiny table: a dirty CSV string, the expected clean CSV, the dirt types we injected, and the column kinds used by the cleaner.

## Cleaning rules (the ground truth)
1. Strip a UTF-8 BOM if present.
2. Normalise header names to snake_case (non-word characters become underscores, lowercased). If two headers collide, suffix _2, _3, ...
3. Trim leading and trailing whitespace on every field; collapse internal runs of spaces to one; treat non-breaking spaces as spaces.
4. Treat these (case-insensitive) as empty: empty string, NA, N/A, null, none, nil, nan, and a lone hyphen.
5. Dates (kind=date): parse %Y-%m-%d, %m/%d/%Y, %d-%b-%Y, %b %d, %Y, %Y/%m/%d, %d.%m.%Y, or ISO, and emit YYYY-MM-DD. Unparseable non-empty leftover would be a generator bug (none in this set).
6. Money (kind=money): strip $, USD and thousands separators; quantize to two decimal places, half-even.
7. Booleans (kind=bool): true/t/yes/y/1 -> true; false/f/no/n/0 -> false (case-insensitive, trimmed).
8. Integers (kind=int): strip commas, emit a base-10 integer string.
9. Emails (kind=email): lowercase.
10. Categories (kind=category): lowercase.
11. Drop exact-duplicate rows after cleaning, keeping the first.

The published clean_csv is the output of this same cleaner. A copy of the script ships with the full pack.
